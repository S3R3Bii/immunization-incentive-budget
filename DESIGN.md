# What would justify paying for incentives?

A program officer can fund reliable monthly camps and is considering an incentive for families. Compare otherwise similar camps with and without incentives in the Banerjee, Duflo, Glennerster and Kothari village trial in rural Rajasthan. The choice of question is informed by the published results.

Use the endline survey's direct study villages, ages 12 through 36 months, and the authors' five-or-more shots definition. Recover all three arm counts against the corrected public abstract. Separate observations missing the outcome. Publish only grouped counts with no geographic or participant identifiers. Retain separate village counts for the sensitivity to weighting and village-level uncertainty.

Estimate the incremental difference in completion per eligible child for the incentive over a reliable camp. Pool children for the primary contrast. Also give every participating village equal weight and omit one village at a time. Approximate sampling uncertainty at village level. Report the difference from a camp-only baseline, distinct from the effect of offering a camp where none existed.

Translate this effect to maximum incremental dollars per eligible child using an explicit funder ceiling and an assumed fraction of trial impact retained. These planning inputs are illustrative. Require a local incremental budget, a credible local comparison, and a count of eligible children before recommending spending. The archived trial's cost per fully immunized child has a different denominator.
